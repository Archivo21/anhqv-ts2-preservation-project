# Financial Transparency and Records Policy

## Purpose

The project should be able to answer:

- where money came from;
- whether it was restricted;
- what it was used for;
- who benefited;
- what evidence exists;
- who reviewed the decision;
- how much remains.

## Ledger

The working ledger should record at least:

- transaction ID;
- date;
- payer or payee;
- description;
- income or expenditure;
- amount and currency;
- category;
- restricted or unrestricted status;
- related project activity;
- evidence location;
- decision-record link;
- conflict status;
- running balance.

## Evidence Storage

Original receipts, invoices, statements, and contracts may contain private data and should not automatically be committed to the public repository.

The public record may contain:

- redacted evidence;
- a checksum;
- an evidence identifier;
- a summary;
- the private storage location known to authorised stewards.

## Reporting Frequency

When no money has been received or spent, a simple status statement is sufficient.

Once financial activity begins, the project should publish:

- periodic summaries at a frequency proportionate to activity;
- at least one annual transparency report;
- an additional report after a major campaign, grant, or material commitment.

## Public Report Content

Reports should include:

- opening and closing balance;
- income by source type;
- expenditure by category;
- restricted-fund movements;
- contributor compensation and support totals;
- declared conflicts;
- reserve purpose and balance;
- significant liabilities or commitments;
- unresolved reconciliation items.

Individual personal details may be aggregated or redacted where disclosure would be disproportionate.

## Corrections

Financial errors must be corrected openly.

Do not erase an incorrect entry without preserving:

- the original transaction ID;
- the correction;
- the reason;
- the reviewer;
- the date.

## Retention

Records should be retained for at least as long as required by applicable law, platform terms, grants, contracts, or professional advice.

Where no shorter lawful schedule has been adopted, preserve records indefinitely when practical because they form part of the project's institutional history.

## Access

The Funds Custodian must have access to complete records.

Reviewing stewards receive the access needed for scrutiny, subject to privacy and security.

Public access is provided through redacted reports rather than unrestricted publication of bank details, addresses, or identity documents.
