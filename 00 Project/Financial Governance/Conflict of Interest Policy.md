# Conflict of Interest Policy

## Principle

A conflict of interest is not automatically wrongdoing.

The risk arises when a personal, family, professional, financial, reputational, or platform interest is hidden or allowed to distort a decision.

## Who Must Declare

This policy applies to:

- moderators;
- financial stewards;
- contributors recommending expenditure;
- reviewers;
- contractors;
- anyone influencing the use of restricted funds.

## Examples

A conflict may exist when a decision concerns:

- payment to the decision-maker;
- food, rent, utilities, subscriptions, or welfare support benefiting a founder;
- a company, platform, or service connected to a contributor;
- hiring a friend, relative, or existing collaborator;
- sponsorship from an organisation being researched or criticised;
- donor conditions that could affect conclusions;
- use of a contributor's own equipment, premises, or intellectual property.

## Required Disclosure

A disclosure should record:

- the person;
- the nature of the interest;
- the proposed decision;
- the likely benefit or risk;
- the method used to reduce bias;
- who reviewed the matter.

## Managing a Conflict

Possible safeguards include:

- independent human review;
- Archivo 21's documented advisory review;
- comparison with alternative prices or methods;
- a pre-approved budget;
- public disclosure;
- a capped or time-limited arrangement;
- retrospective review;
- abstention where another qualified decision-maker exists.

## Founder and Custodian Payments

The project explicitly permits payments benefiting founders when they support project work or continuity.

Such payments must not be concealed.

They should be classified as one of:

- direct operating expense;
- mixed-use project allocation;
- reimbursement;
- compensation;
- stipend;
- continuity support.

A label should describe the real purpose, not produce a more convenient appearance.

## Donor Conflicts

A donation or sponsorship must be refused, returned, or restricted when its conditions would require the project to:

- falsify or suppress evidence;
- grant undisclosed control;
- misrepresent affiliation;
- violate rights or privacy;
- abandon core preservation principles.

## Confidentiality

Public reports should disclose enough to explain the conflict and decision without exposing unnecessary banking, health, housing, or other sensitive personal information.
