# Contributor Roles

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Active

## Purpose

This document defines mission roles within the ANHQV × The Sims 2 Preservation Project.

A role describes what a contributor contributes—not their authority, identity, prestige, or implementation.

A contributor may fulfil several roles. Several contributors may fulfil the same role.

## Roles, Permissions, and Identity

These concepts are independent:

- **Identity:** what kind of participant they are, such as human, language model, or agent.
- **Mission role:** what they contribute.
- **Permission level:** what they are authorised to do in the repository.

A human and an autonomous system could both be Field Researchers if each can operate a live environment reliably. A recognised ANHQV expert may still begin with limited repository permissions. A Moderator is not automatically a Specialist.

## Dual-Mission Application

Every role may contribute to one or both sides of the project:

- preserving and understanding ANHQV;
- reconstructing and investigating its world through *The Sims 2*.

The project especially values people who help the two communities understand one another.

# Research Colleague

## Mission

Advance understanding through reasoning, discussion, source comparison, and collaborative investigation.

## Primary Responsibilities

- develop hypotheses and research questions;
- design experiments or source investigations;
- challenge assumptions;
- interpret evidence;
- connect ANHQV meaning with Sims 2 implementation;
- improve documentation;
- assist with long-term planning.

## Expected Mindset

Curious, critical, collaborative, and comfortable admitting uncertainty.

## Success

The project consistently asks better questions and reaches better-supported conclusions because this contributor participates.

# Field Researcher

## Mission

Generate new evidence through direct interaction with a live research environment.

At present, the principal environment is *The Sims 2*. Future systems may operate the game, source archives, audiovisual material, or physical-media workflows directly.

## Primary Responsibilities

- perform experiments;
- reproduce reported behaviour;
- record conditions and observations;
- capture screenshots, video, logs, or files;
- collect raw data;
- compare simulation results with ANHQV requirements;
- report unexpected discoveries honestly.

## Expected Mindset

Patient, methodical, observant, and honest about failed or ambiguous results.

## Success

The project gains reliable evidence that previously did not exist.

# Specialist

## Mission

Provide deep knowledge or skilled practice within a clearly defined discipline.

## Example Specialisms

### ANHQV

- episode chronology and continuity;
- characters, relationships, dialogue, and recurring jokes;
- production history, broadcast context, and physical releases;
- locations, sets, architecture, and cultural context;
- source provenance and fan-archive history.

### The Sims 2 and Technical Research

- building and constrainFloorElevation;
- reverse engineering and package analysis;
- graphics, rendering, routing, and cameras;
- LotAdjuster, mods, custom content, and compatibility;
- Git, data organisation, and reproducibility.

### Preservation and Communication

- digital preservation;
- audiovisual restoration and video editing;
- transcription, subtitling, and translation;
- rights and permissions research;
- documentation and information architecture.

## Primary Responsibilities

- answer specialist questions within scope;
- review relevant work;
- identify mistakes and risks;
- recommend methods and standards;
- state the limits of their expertise.

## Success

Their specialist knowledge prevents errors, improves quality, or enables work the wider team could not otherwise complete.

## Video Editor Specialist Profile

A Video Editor Specialist is recruited under the Specialist mission role.

### Responsibilities

- preserve raw recordings unchanged;
- identify evidentially complete excerpts;
- remove irrelevant or private audio without concealing research-relevant events;
- clean, caption, and annotate footage;
- produce accessible public exports;
- maintain an edit decision log;
- distinguish publication footage from the evidence source.

### Success

A viewer can understand the experiment quickly without being misled about what happened, what was cut, or how certain the conclusion is.

# Consultant

## Mission

Provide an independent perspective without necessarily joining day-to-day work.

## Primary Responsibilities

- review work;
- challenge conclusions;
- offer recommendations;
- identify cultural, technical, methodological, or ethical blind spots.

## Expected Mindset

Independent, objective, and constructively sceptical.

## Success

The project avoids tunnel vision.

# Assistant

## Mission

Increase project efficiency through clearly defined tasks.

## Examples

- formatting and proofreading;
- file organisation and conversion;
- transcription and indexing;
- metadata and checksum generation;
- administrative work;
- preparing rights-cleared publication assets.

## Primary Responsibilities

Execute tasks accurately and consistently. Assist rather than direct unless another role also applies.

## Expected Mindset

Reliable, consistent, and attentive to detail.

## Success

Researchers spend more time investigating and less time repeating avoidable administrative work.

## Multiple and Changing Roles

A contributor may act simultaneously as:

- Research Colleague and Field Researcher;
- Specialist and Consultant;
- Specialist and Assistant;
- Research Colleague and Video Editor Specialist.

Roles may develop as capabilities and project needs change. Changes should be documented.

## Creating New Roles

Create a new mission role only when existing roles cannot reasonably contain the required responsibility.

Prefer adding a specialist profile before inventing a new mission role.

## Final Principle

Roles exist to improve collaboration, not establish hierarchy.

The project depends on discovering, interpreting, communicating, documenting, and preserving evidence—and on remembering why the reconstruction matters culturally.
