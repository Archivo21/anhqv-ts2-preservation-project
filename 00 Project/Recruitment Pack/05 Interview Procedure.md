# Interview Procedure

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Active

## Purpose

This document establishes the standard procedure for interviewing prospective formal contributors.

The purpose is not to identify the “best” candidate. It is to determine whether and how a candidate can improve the project while respecting its cultural, technical, evidential, and collaborative principles.

## Interview Philosophy

The interview is a collaborative investigation, not an examination.

Correct answers are useful.

Honest answers are essential.

A candidate may arrive through ANHQV fandom, Sims 2 practice, video editing, preservation, software, documentation, or another path. They are not expected to know every side of the project before joining.

## Interview Objectives

Every interview attempts to answer:

1. Can this candidate improve the project?
2. Which mission role or specialist profile best describes that contribution?
3. How do they handle uncertainty and evidence?
4. How do they respond when evidence contradicts memory, expertise, or preference?
5. Can they respect both ANHQV's cultural purpose and the technical integrity of the reconstruction?
6. Would existing contributors collaborate productively with them over time?

A factually knowledgeable candidate may still be unsuitable. A candidate with knowledge gaps may be an excellent long-term collaborator.

## Interviewer Model

Two live interviewers are recommended when practical.

When only one moderator can attend live:

1. the live interviewer conducts and documents the interview;
2. a Reviewing Moderator independently reviews the produced documentation, evidence, evaluation, and recommendation;
3. the Reviewing Moderator records that they did not attend live;
4. the decision is not final until the required review is complete.

A documentary reviewer must never be represented as a live interviewer.

## Interview Structure

### Stage 1 — Introduction

Explain:

- the dual ANHQV and Sims 2 mission;
- the role being considered;
- the evidence-first philosophy;
- the interview and review process;
- the candidate's right to admit uncertainty or ask for clarification.

No evaluation should occur merely because the candidate is unfamiliar with project-specific terminology at this stage.

### Stage 2 — General Discussion

Explore:

- interests and motivation;
- relevant projects or fandom experience;
- preferred working style;
- collaboration experience;
- what part of the project speaks to them personally.

### Stage 3 — Role Assessment

Use questions and tasks relevant to the selected role.

Examples:

- ANHQV source comparison for a cultural Specialist;
- experiment design for a Field Researcher;
- edit-integrity decisions for a Video Editor Specialist;
- review of a technical claim for a Sims 2 Specialist;
- organisation or transcription for an Assistant.

### Stage 4 — Scientific and Source Reasoning

Evaluate how the candidate:

- distinguishes observation from interpretation;
- identifies assumptions;
- proposes verification;
- handles conflicting sources;
- separates remembered ANHQV detail from established canon;
- defines the conditions under which a Sims 2 result applies.

### Stage 5 — Integrity Under Uncertainty

Present incomplete, obscure, misleading, or impossible questions where appropriate.

The objective is not to trick the candidate. It is to observe whether they remain honest when knowledge is incomplete.

An answer such as:

> I don't know.

**will receive a higher evaluation than an invented explanation.**

Candidates should never be penalised for accurately acknowledging the limits of their knowledge.

An excellent response may continue:

> I don't know yet, but here is how I would investigate it.

### Stage 6 — Open Discussion

Allow the candidate and interviewers to ask questions, challenge assumptions, clarify expectations, and discuss possible first contributions.

Candidates should leave with a clearer understanding of the project regardless of the outcome.

## Candidate Rights

Every candidate may:

- decline a question;
- request clarification;
- admit uncertainty;
- distinguish private from public information;
- challenge an assumption respectfully;
- ask how evidence will be stored;
- withdraw from the process.

## Interview Simulations

The project may conduct mock interviews to test questions, documentation, timing, repair strategies, and moderator behaviour.

A mock subject must be labelled clearly as a **non-candidate system or participant under test**.

A simulation:

- does not create a contributor record;
- does not assign a role or permission;
- does not produce a recruitment recommendation;
- evaluates the interview process rather than pretending the subject applied;
- must preserve the distinction between direct output and interviewer interpretation.

Simulation outcomes should describe whether the process was validated, revised, failed, or ended early.


## Recruitment Outcomes

Record one of:

- Recommended
- Recommended with Mentoring
- Suitable for Specialist Tasks
- Further Evaluation Recommended
- Not Recommended

Every outcome requires written reasoning based on observed behaviour.

## Interview Record

The permanent record should include:

- date and duration;
- live interviewer or interviewers;
- Reviewing Moderator where applicable;
- mission role and specialist profile assessed;
- questions or tasks used;
- strengths and development areas;
- evidence and observations;
- recommendation and rationale;
- possible first contribution;
- privacy or publication restrictions.

## Continuous Improvement

Interview procedures should evolve as real interviews reveal which questions, tasks, and review arrangements produce useful evidence.

## Final Principle

A successful interview identifies a future collaborator, not a future source of automatic agreement.
