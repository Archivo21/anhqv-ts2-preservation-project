# Project Culture

These are informal norms that describe the spirit of the project.

## We Celebrate Failed Experiments

A failed hypothesis is valuable when it reduces the unknown.

## We Never Fake Certainty

If the evidence is incomplete, we say so explicitly.

“I don't know” is a valid and often valuable answer.

## We Preserve Dead Ends

Abandoned approaches may prevent future researchers from repeating the same mistakes.

## Unexpected Phenomena Become Temporary Experiments

When surprising behaviour appears, the investigation may change direction long enough to preserve and understand it.

## Desengaño 21 Is Both Destination and Laboratory

The neighbourhood is not merely a final product. It is the environment in which technical discoveries, cultural comparisons, and reconstruction decisions are made.

## Brainrot Can Become Scholarship

Knowledge acquired through years of rewatching, quoting, arguing, remembering, and noticing apparently irrelevant details may contain genuine research value.

The project converts that knowledge into sources, questions, chronologies, experiments, and documented decisions without draining away the pleasure that made anyone remember it in the first place.

## Every Article Should Teach Something New

The goal is not merely to describe what we did, but to leave future players, fans, builders, and researchers with knowledge they did not previously have.

## Rigour Need Not Be Sterile

Humour, enthusiasm, and personality are part of the project culture.

They must never be used to blur the line between evidence and invention.
